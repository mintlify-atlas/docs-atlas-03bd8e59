Once the backend returns a successful response, the input form is replaced by the **Results Panel** — a scrollable list of vehicle cards followed by export controls at the bottom. Each card presents a complete picture of one vehicle's route: a header with load utilization, two summary blocks for driving and walking totals, and a detailed stop-by-stop timeline table. Routes start at **06:00** and all times cascade forward from that anchor.

## Vehicle Summary Cards

Every optimized vehicle that was assigned at least one stop gets its own card. The dark header row shows the vehicle name, its internal ID, and a load indicator in the top-right corner.

```text
Vehicle 1                          Total Load
ID: <uuid>              MAPS 🚚    15 / 15
```

The load indicator displays `route_load / vehicle.capacity`. When the numerator equals the denominator the vehicle is fully loaded; partial loads are normal and expected when OR-Tools drops nodes below the penalty threshold.

### Summary Blocks

Below the header, two metric blocks sit side by side:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Driving Summary" icon="truck">
    **Total driving distance** in kilometers (`total_driving_distance / 1000`) and **total driving time** formatted as `HH:MM hrs` or `MM min`. These values represent the sum of all road-network segments returned by ORS for the vehicle's route.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Walking Summary" icon="person-walking">
    **Total walking distance** (`total_walking_distance × 2 / 1000` km) and **total walking time** (`total_walking_time × 2`), both doubled to reflect a delivery-person round-trip from station to customer and back. This block is hidden entirely when the vehicle has no walking segments.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  Walking distance and time are multiplied by **2** in the UI because the delivery person must walk from the parking station to the customer and return to the vehicle. The raw values stored in `total_walking_distance` and `total_walking_time` are one-way figures from ORS.
</Note>

## Stop Timeline Table

The timeline table lists every stop in the vehicle's route from departure at the depot through the return trip, with one row per main stop. All times are clock-formatted in 12-hour notation (e.g. `08:45`).

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `#` | Zero-based stop index in the route sequence |
| `Type` | `DEPOT`, `HUB`, or `STOP` |
| `Location Name` | Node name in bold, address in smaller grey text below |
| `Accum. Dist.` | Cumulative driving distance from the depot in km |
| Package icon | Demand (load units) at this stop |
| `Transp. Duration` | Travel time from the **previous** stop; `--` for the first depot row |
| `Arrival` | Clock time when the vehicle arrives; `--` for the first depot row |
| `Departure` | Clock time when the vehicle leaves; `--` for the final depot return row |
| `Service Time` | Minutes spent working at the stop (`working_time` field); `--` for DEPOT and HUB rows |

Row background colors communicate the stop type at a glance:

- **Blue row** — DEPOT (start/end of route)
- **Yellow-tinted row** — HUB (EZ Parking station; vehicle waits while deliveries are made on foot)
- **White row** — regular customer STOP

## Walking Sub-Rows

When a vehicle visits an EZ Parking station (a HUB row), the stops served on foot from that hub are rendered as **indented sub-rows** directly below the HUB row. These `↳ WALKING` rows share the same columns as main rows but with a few differences:

- **`#` column** shows `↳` instead of an index
- **`Accum. Dist.`** shows `+ X.XX km (x2)` — the one-way walk distance with the round-trip multiplier noted
- **`Arrival`** and **`Departure`** are the clock times for that individual customer walk leg
- Time is computed sequentially: each walk leg begins when the previous walk leg's departure time ends, so the vehicle accumulates waiting time for the full station-visit duration

<Tip>
  The HUB row's `Departure` time reflects when the vehicle leaves after **all** walking legs are complete, not just the first customer. Check the last walking sub-row's `Departure` to see when the delivery person returns to the vehicle.
</Tip>

## Unassigned Nodes Panel

If OR-Tools could not fit one or more customer nodes into any vehicle route, they appear in a **red-bordered panel** at the bottom of the results list, titled **Unassigned Nodes (N)**.

Each unassigned node shows its name, address, and load. A node is unassigned when the cost of routing to it exceeds the solver's drop penalty of 10,000 time-units — this typically happens when:

- **All vehicles are at capacity** and the remaining demand cannot fit in any route
- **A single node's demand exceeds every vehicle's capacity**, making it physically impossible to serve
- **The node is geographically isolated**, making its inclusion more costly than the penalty

<Warning>
  Unassigned nodes represent real customers that will **not** be served by the generated routes. Review them carefully before acting on the results. To resolve unassigned nodes: add one or more vehicles, increase vehicle capacities, or reduce demand values where appropriate.
</Warning>

## Exporting Results

Two download buttons appear at the bottom of the results panel, and each vehicle card also provides a direct Google Maps link in its header:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Export Raw Nodes">
    Clicking **Export Raw Nodes** downloads `nodes_generated.csv` — a comma-separated file containing the complete node list as processed by the backend, including any `nearby_node_id` assignments that the proximity step added.

    **Columns:** `name`, `address`, `lat`, `lng`, `load`, `working_time`

    This file is useful for auditing geocoding results or re-importing a corrected node set on a subsequent run.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Export Routing Table">
    Clicking **Export Routing Table** downloads `routing_results_table.csv` — a row-per-stop export of the full stop timeline, including walking sub-rows.

    **Columns:** `Vehicle`, `Stop #`, `Type`, `Location Name`, `Accum. Dist. (km)`, `Load`, `Transp. Duration`, `Arrival`, `Departure`, `Service Time`

    Walking sub-rows are included with `↳` in the `Stop #` column and `WALKING` in the `Type` column, matching the visual table exactly.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Open in Google Maps">
    Each vehicle card has a **MAPS** button in its header. Clicking it opens a new browser tab (or the Google Maps app on mobile) with a driving directions URL pre-populated with:
    - **Origin:** depot coordinates
    - **Destination:** depot coordinates (return trip)
    - **Waypoints:** all intermediate stops as `lat,lng` pairs

    The URL uses the `https://www.google.com/maps/dir/?api=1&...` deep-link format with `travelmode=driving`.

    <Warning>
      Google Maps supports a maximum of **9 intermediate waypoints** in a single directions URL. If a vehicle route has more than 9 intermediate stops, the UI will show an alert noting that Google Maps may truncate the route, but the first 9 waypoints will be rendered correctly.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Return to Home

The **Return to Home** button appears in the top-right corner of the sidebar header whenever results are displayed. Clicking it calls `setRouteData(null)`, which clears the optimization response and restores the input form with your original nodes and vehicles still in place — so you can adjust parameters and re-run without re-entering data from scratch.
